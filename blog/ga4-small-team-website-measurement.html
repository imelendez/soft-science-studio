What should a small team measure in GA4? Start with inquiries

By Ismael Melendez

For a small service business or nonprofit team, website measurement should begin with a practical question: is the site helping people take the next useful step?

Google Analytics 4, usually called GA4, helps describe activity on your website. You do not need a complicated dashboard to get started. First decide what the website is meant to help someone do.

Use three views of the journey

Google Search Console helps explain how people encounter your pages in Google Search. GA4 helps explain activity after visitors reach your website. Your inquiry form or contact records tell you which messages actually arrived.

These views serve different purposes. Their counts will not necessarily match because their definitions, coverage, and collection methods differ. Treat them as related evidence rather than a single perfect record of each person.

Separate interest from an inquiry

A click on “Tell me about your project” shows interest. A submitted inquiry means someone provided a way to continue the conversation. A booked call or signed project happens later.

GA4's recommended event for a generated lead is called generate_lead. Use it when a real lead is confirmed, not when someone merely clicks toward a form. An important recorded action can be marked as a key event in GA4. See Google's recommended events and key event guidance.

If your form is embedded from Google Forms

Keep the first setup simple. Track clicks from your website toward Contact and the direct form link. Use the form's recorded responses to count actual inquiries.

An embedded Google Form runs on a different origin from your website. Ordinary tracking code on the surrounding page cannot read its submission confirmation. Loading the Contact page, viewing the iframe, or clicking the form link should not be reported as a completed lead.

You can still learn which articles lead people toward Contact. Just label that result accurately as interest rather than form completion.

Label links you share outside the website

Campaign parameters, often called UTM tags, help identify incoming links. For example, use one consistent campaign name when sharing an article on LinkedIn or in a newsletter. Keep the labels simple and avoid tagging your site's internal navigation. Google's campaign URL guidance

Never put a person's name or email address into campaign labels or send form-message contents to GA4. Google's guidance on personal information

Review the evidence without overreacting

At an early stage, review search discovery, visits to useful pages, contact clicks, and recorded inquiries. Keep a separate note of whether an inquiry fits your services and becomes a proposal or project.

A few visits are not enough to declare an article a success or failure. Use the numbers alongside the questions people ask you, and change the page when you can explain what problem that change should solve.
