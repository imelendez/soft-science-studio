Your campaign brought more visitors. What happened next?

By Ismael Melendez

More visitors can be a promising campaign result. It still leaves a practical question: what did those people do when they arrived?

In a 2024 nonprofit influencer campaign analysis, I brought together social-platform data and website activity to examine how the campaign performed. The work involved an audience reached through gaming and streaming communities.

The project summary describes a substantial increase in website users during campaign activity, alongside a decline in average engagement time. Both findings mattered. Presenting only the traffic increase would have left out an important part of the picture.

The question behind the dashboard

The purpose of the analysis was to understand the visitor journey, not simply collect impressive totals. A campaign can attract attention while its landing page leaves people unsure what to do next.

Lower average engagement time alone does not prove that visitors were uninterested. Some people may find an answer quickly. Others may leave because the page does not match the promise that brought them there. The available summary does not establish which explanation applied to each visitor.

That uncertainty is a reason to investigate the page and its intended action together.

What I recommended

My report recommended simplifying calls to action, creating landing pages suited to individual influencers, and making campaign-link tagging more consistent.

A call to action is the next step you ask someone to take: read a resource, sign up, or contact the team. When a page asks for several competing actions, it can become harder to understand its purpose.

Campaign tags are labels added to incoming links so reporting can distinguish sources. Consistent labels make comparisons easier to interpret. These were recommendations from the analysis; this article does not claim they were all implemented or produced a measured conversion increase.

How I would approach the measurement today

For a current campaign, I would define the intended action before launch and verify how it is recorded in GA4 or the destination platform. I would also check whether people must leave the website to complete it.

That distinction matters for a signup form hosted elsewhere: a click toward the form is evidence of interest, while a recorded submission is evidence that someone completed it.

What this means for your team

A useful campaign report helps you decide what to change. It explains which audiences arrived, what they encountered, and where the evidence stops.

If your report ends with visitor totals, start by adding the next question: what action did this campaign need people to take?
