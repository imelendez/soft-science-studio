A new contact system needs a plan for the people using it

By Ismael Melendez

A new contact system can look capable in a demonstration and still be difficult to use in everyday work. Staff need to know what to enter, where it belongs, and who can help when something looks wrong.

While supporting a nonprofit's Qomon rollout, I worked across organizing staff, the platform vendor, and a technical partner. My work included gathering requirements, comparing features, supporting onboarding, and following up on whether information moved correctly between systems.

Start with the work people need to do

A CRM is a system for keeping track of contacts and relationships. For an organizing team, that can include conversations, participation, and follow-up.

The evaluation used a comparison matrix to review vendor features against team needs. That gave the discussion a structure beyond which platform looked most appealing.

For a team evaluating its own options, I would start with a few ordinary tasks. Can an organizer find a contact, record a conversation, and identify the next action? Can someone responsible for reporting understand the resulting records?

Make room for practice and questions

I helped facilitate recurring “data input parties”: working sessions where organizers could enter information, ask questions, and troubleshoot together.

The sessions made support part of the rollout. My work also included coordinating changes to custom fields and following up with partners about data syncing with Action Network.

These activities matter because a system can accept an entry without making that entry useful elsewhere. A field name, category, or missing transfer can affect the report another team relies on.

Plan the handoff

The project summary records a transition of support documentation and vendor relationships to an incoming data manager. That is part of making a tool maintainable: someone needs to know how decisions were made and where unresolved questions belong.

I am describing a supported rollout and coordination role, not claiming sole ownership of the platform implementation or a quantified organization-wide adoption increase.

What a small team can borrow

Before choosing another tool, map one workflow from entry to follow-up to reporting. Identify the people involved, the fields they need, and the checks that show information is moving as expected.

Then reserve time for staff to practice with realistic examples. A short guide and a named support owner can be more immediately useful than a long feature list.
