Services

Four straightforward ways to work together.

Most work starts with a conversation about what's actually slowing your team down. You'll receive a clear scope, timeline, and price before work begins.

Tell me about your project
01

Get a clear plan

When this fits: You know something isn't working — the numbers take too long to pull, two systems disagree, or you're not sure whether to fix your current setup or replace it — but you want an outside read before committing budget.

What you get: A working session to walk through your current setup, followed by a short written plan: what's actually causing the problem, the realistic options with their trade-offs, and what I'd do first if it were my call.

02

Fix or build a reporting workflow

When this fits: A recurring report eats days of staff time, your dashboard is stale or wrong, program and donation data live in separate places, or your contact records have grown inconsistent enough that mailings and reports can't be trusted.

What you get: A working setup — a rebuilt report, dashboard, cleaned and reconciled data, or an automated process that replaces the manual one — plus written documentation so your team can run and maintain it after I'm done.

03

Train your team

When this fits: You have the tools but not the confidence — staff avoid the dashboard, the database gets used inconsistently, or people want to use AI tools at work without a clear sense of what's safe and useful.

What you get: A practical session built around your actual tools and data, not generic slides. Common topics: reading and trusting your reports, keeping contact records clean and consistent, website and campaign measurement, spreadsheet and data fundamentals, and practical AI use for everyday work.

04

Get ongoing data and technology support

When this fits: You need experienced technical judgment available regularly, but nowhere near a full-time hire — the classic small-organization situation where data questions land on whoever is least busy that week.

What you get: Ongoing support without a full-time hire: regular check-in time, a prioritized list of improvements worked through over time, help troubleshooting and evaluating vendors, and someone to think through technology decisions with before you commit to them.

Marketing & campaign measurement

Know which channels actually bring people in.

A common starting point: website tracking that records what visitors actually do, campaign reporting that holds up across email, social, and events, and a clear read on which channels generate signups, donations, and inquiries — so budget and volunteer hours go where they're working.
